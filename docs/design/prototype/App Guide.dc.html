A friendly guide, not a manual

What is this app, and what can it do for you?

This app is a place to write down what happens to your money — what you earned, what you spent, what you moved, and what you lent to people who owe it back. It doesn't connect to your bank and it doesn't do anything automatically. Everything in it is something you told it.

In return, it helps you answer four simple questions, honestly and without judgement:

Your money
Where is my money?
Accounts
What happened to it?
Transactions
What's coming up?
Plan & regular bills
What's owed to me?
Money Lent

Reports pull all four together and describe your own habits back to you — nothing here is advice, and nothing is ever automatic.

Where your money is

Accounts

An account is simply a place your money is kept or tracked — a bank, a wallet, cash in your pocket, a credit card, even money you've lent to someone. The app doesn't connect to any of these; you tell it what's in each one, and it keeps the running total from there.

What you'll find under Accounts
Everyday accountsBank account, Cash, Wallet — money you actually have and can spend.
What you oweCredit card, home loan — accounts that count against you, shown as what's still owed.
Money lentA running tally of everything people currently owe you (see the Money Lent section below).
Somewhere elseA holding spot for money you've moved out to something untracked, like an investment.

Money you've lent counts toward what you have, the same way a loan you owe counts against you — lending doesn't change your net worth, only who's holding it.

How do I check a balance is right?

Open an account and tap Check this balance. Enter what you actually counted — if it matches, mark it checked. If it doesn't, the app offers to record the gap as spending it missed, so your numbers stay honest without you hunting for a lost receipt.

What does "Private" mean?

A private account (like your salary bank account by default) is left out of the totals shown on Home and Plan. Its balance is still visible if you open Accounts directly — it's hidden from summaries, not locked away.

Can I add or archive an account?

Yes — tap Add an account from the bottom of the list, or open an account and choose Archive or rename. An account with no history can be deleted outright; one with history can only be archived, so old transactions still make sense.

What happened to your money

Transactions

A transaction is one recorded event: money that came in, money that went out, or money that moved between two of your own accounts. Every one lives on the Transactions tab, newest first, grouped by day.

Received
Salary, a refund, a side payment. Example: Salary → +₹50,000
Spent
Groceries, rent, a bill. Example: Groceries → −₹1,200
Moved
Between two accounts you own. Example: Bank → Savings, ₹10,000
An important distinction

Moving ₹10,000 from your bank to your savings is not spending ₹10,000 — it's the same money, just in a different place.

Bank
₹50,000
→ ₹10,000 →
Savings
₹10,000

You still have ₹60,000 total. The money simply changed location.

How do I record something?

Tap the round + button. Choose Spent, Received, or Moved, type the amount on the number pad, pick a category (and account), then save. Payee and a note are optional, tucked under "Show payee and note."

What if I bought something and later got money back?

Open the original purchase and choose Mark money came back — partial or full. It reduces that category's spending for the month; it is never counted as separate income.

Can one payment cover two categories?

Yes — open the transaction and choose Split across categories. You divide the amount across up to four categories; the parts always add back up to the original, to the rupee.

What does a transaction's detail screen show?

When it happened, the category, payee, which account it touched, any note, whether it's a must-pay or flexible cost, and whether it's linked to a bill, a split, or a refund. From there you can edit, delete, duplicate to today, or mark it a one-off.

Making sense of it

Income, spending, and categories

Income is money you receive — salary, a side project, a refund. Spending is money you actually use to buy something. Every spending transaction gets a category: a label like "Food & Groceries" or "Bills & Utilities" that says what it was for.

You can create your own too — tap Create a category from the Categories screen. Renaming one keeps every past transaction attached to it.

What does "Must-pay" vs "Flexible" mean?

Some categories — rent, bills, health, EMI — are marked Must-pay, money you'd struggle to skip. Everything else is Flexible, spending you choose month to month. Reports split totals by these two groups so you can see how much of your money is actually optional.

What does "repeats" mean on a category?

It's a hint that spending in that category tends to happen every month — useful context, separate from Regular Bills (see Plan, below), which actually reminds you of specific due dates.

How do categories show up elsewhere?

Reports break every period down by category, biggest first. Spending limits (in Plan) are set per category. And two special categories appear automatically: Interest & charges for loan interest, and Not recorded for gaps a balance check turns up.

Your financial snapshot

Home (the dashboard)

Home is the first thing you see when you open the app. It's built to answer "where do I stand right now?" in a glance, without digging through menus.

Money available

The total across your everyday accounts (private accounts, if any, are left out of this figure — and it says so). Tap it to open Accounts.

Left to spend this month

Money available, minus bills still to come this month, minus a buffer you've chosen to keep back. This is the number most worth watching day to day.

Spent so far this month

Compared, once you have enough history, against a typical month by the same day — "₹3,400 more than a typical month by day 16," for example.

One insight card

A single, changing observation — a category running unusually high, a bill that got more expensive, spending recorded is looking thin. Each comes with an action, and a "Not useful" button if it isn't.

In your first month, you'll see facts only — no comparisons yet. That's deliberate: the app waits until it has enough of your own history (three complete months of recording) before comparing anything, so it's never guessing.

Deciding ahead of time

Plan: bills and spending limits

The Plan tab is where you look forward instead of back. It has two ideas: regular bills, things you already know are coming, and spending limits, a target you set for yourself.

The maths behind "left to spend"
Money available₹40,000
− Bills still to come this month−₹18,000
− Buffer you asked to keep back−₹2,000
= Left to spend₹20,000
What's a regular bill?

A memory aid for things that repeat — rent, a subscription, an electricity bill — with a name, category, rough amount, how often, and a due day. The app never pays it for you; it just matches it against a real transaction you record with a similar payee and amount near the due date, so you can see at a glance what's paid and what's still coming.

How do I set a spending limit?

In Plan, open Spending limits, pick a category without a limit yet, and set an amount. From day 5 of the month, the app shows whether you're on pace, ahead of pace, or already over — but a limit is a target you set for yourself, never something the app enforces or blocks.

What happens if I go over a limit?

Nothing is stopped. The bar for that category turns amber and says how much over you are — purely informational, so you can decide what to do about it.

What's the "buffer"?

A cushion you choose to hold back from "left to spend," for peace of mind. Change it any time from Preferences.

Example

You decide you want to spend no more than ₹8,000 on Food this month. Two weeks in you've spent ₹5,200 — the limit bar shows ₹2,800 remaining and "On pace." Nothing is blocked either way; it's simply there for you to check.

Money others owe you

Money Lent

The single most important thing to understand: money you lend someone is not the same as money you spend.

When you lend money, it leaves your account, but it doesn't disappear — it becomes something someone owes you, which the app calls a receivable. It still counts as part of what you have; it's just being held by someone else for now.

How do I add a loan?

Settings → Accounts → Money Lent → Add a loan. Enter who you lent it to, how much, which account it came from, and whether it's interest-free or carries a rate. You can add an optional due date and a note.

How do repayments work?

Open the loan and choose Record repayment, then enter the amount — or tap "Pay in full" for the exact total owed. If interest has built up, the app pays that off first, then reduces the original amount; you never have to work out the split yourself.

What does each loan status mean?

Active — nothing repaid yet. Partially repaid — some has come back. Fully repaid — nothing outstanding. Overdue — past its due date with a balance remaining. Written off — you've marked it as lost.

What if the money is never coming back?

Open the loan and choose Write off this loan. The outstanding amount is recorded as a real expense (under "Lending write-off"), since it genuinely won't return — this is the one case where a loan does eventually become a loss.

Does interest count as income?

Only once it's actually repaid — interest that has merely accrued isn't counted as money you've made until it's really in your hands.

Reading your own history

Reports & charts

Reports read your own numbers back to you — never advice, never a verdict. Everything here can be filtered by This Month, Last 3 Months, Last 6 Months, 1 Year, or All Time.

Overview
Answers: how much did I spend, where did it go, and what's the trend?

Money in vs out for the period, every category as a bar (biggest first), a must-pay/flexible split, a line chart of income vs expenses over time, and a daily spending calendar you can tap into.

Where it goes
Answers: which few categories eat most of my spending?

Your top three categories as a share of total spending, plus the full category breakdown for any month.

Limits
Answers: am I on track against the limits I set?

Every limit as a bar — how much spent, how much left, and whether you're ahead of, on, or behind pace this month.

What changed
Answers: what's different about this month compared with normal?

Categories that moved noticeably against your own typical month, and any that have been steadily rising or falling for several months in a row. Needs at least three good months of history before it appears.

Regular bills
Answers: what repeats, what's due, and has anything gone up in price?

Every bill this month with its status — paid, due, or overdue — and a heads-up whenever one has gotten more expensive.

Drifting categories
Answers: what's slowly changed over months, even if no single month stood out?

Categories that have steadily crept up or down over six months — the kind of change too gradual to notice month to month.

A monthly review appears automatically once a month closes, summarising it in one place. Any report can be saved as a PDF from the button at the bottom of Reports.

Finding things

Search, filters, and fixing mistakes

"How do I find everything I spent on food last month?"
  1. Open the Transactions tab
  2. Tap the category row and choose Food & Groceries
  3. Tap the "Last month" chip in the filter row

You can also type into the search box — it matches payee, note, category name, or the amount itself — and filter by Spent, Received, Moved, Came back, a specific account, or a specific payee at the same time.

What can I edit?

Almost anything on a transaction — amount, category, account, date, payee, note — by opening it and tapping Edit, which reopens the same entry screen pre-filled.

What happens when I delete something?

A confirmation appears first. Once deleted, most actions offer a brief Undo in a small toast at the bottom of the screen.

What if a whole account balance looks wrong?

Use Check this balance on that account (see Accounts, above) rather than editing individual transactions — it finds the gap and records it in one step, filed under "Not recorded" so it never hides inside a real category.

Can I mark a one-off as not typical?

Yes — open the transaction and choose Mark as a one-off. It's then left out of "typical month" comparisons elsewhere, so a single unusual purchase doesn't skew every future comparison.

Putting it together

What can I do in real life?

"I just received my salary."

Tap + → Received → enter the amount → category "Salary" → choose the account it landed in → Save.

"I bought groceries."

Tap + → Spent → enter the amount → category "Food & Groceries" → the account you paid from → Save.

"I moved money to savings."

Tap + → Moved → enter the amount → From your bank, To savings → Save. This never counts as spending.

"I lent ₹5,000 to my friend."

Settings → Accounts → Money Lent → Add a loan → name your friend, ₹5,000, choose interest-free or a rate, pick the account it came from → Save.

"My friend paid me back ₹2,000."

Open their loan under Money Lent → Record repayment → ₹2,000 → Save. Interest owed, if any, is settled first automatically.

"I want to know where I spent the most this month."

Reports → Overview → pick Last 3 Months (or longer) → tap a month under Period breakdown. Your categories appear as bars, biggest first.

"I want to know how much money I have."

Home shows "Money available" at the top, or open Accounts for the full breakdown, including what's owed to you and what you owe.

Easy to get wrong

A few common mistakes

Don't record a transfer as spending.

Moving money between your own accounts should always use "Moved," not "Spent" — otherwise your spending totals look bigger than they really are.

Don't record money you lend a friend as an expense.

Use Add a loan under Money Lent — it leaves your everyday spending totals untouched and tracks the amount as something owed to you instead.

Don't add the same repayment twice.

Each loan's repayment history is visible on its detail screen — check there before recording another one.

Don't ignore a balance check gap.

If your actual bank balance doesn't match what the app expects, use "Check this balance" rather than editing old transactions to force a match — it keeps a clean, honest trail.

A few words, plainly

Small glossary

Balance — how much money is currently recorded in an account.
Transaction — a record of money coming in, going out, or moving between accounts.
Receivable — money someone else owes you; what's tracked under Money Lent.
Interest — extra money owed because money was lent for a period of time, growing daily on an interest-bearing loan.
Outstanding — how much of a loan or bill still hasn't been paid.
Typical month — the middle value from your own recent history, used for honest comparisons instead of guesses.
